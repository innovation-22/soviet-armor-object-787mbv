Major Modernization Project: Object 787M BV "Gadyuka-MBV" (Modernized Viper)

           Overview of the Modernization Concept The (Object 787MBV) 

• is a radical re-engineering of the 1996 fire-support prototype. Designed for intense urban warfare and the saturation of light armored and aerial targets,
• this package eliminates the sensor and sustained-fire limitations of the original model, transforming the chassis into a platform for absolute suppression without compromising the turret profile.

                  Primary Firepower (Kinetic Saturation)

• Dual 2A72 Main Cannons (30 mm): Retained in a side-by-side turret configuration, but featuring reinforced breech mechanisms to withstand extreme pressure.
• High Rate of Fire: The combined firing rate has been increased from 100 to 250 rounds per second (15,000 rounds per minute for the dual system),
• creating a continuous curtain of fire capable of destroying medium-weight armor and intercepting drones or helicopters.

• Feed System and Capacity: The feed system has been equipped with an expanded drum holding 850 rounds, ready for continuous use before requiring a reload.
• New APFSDS Ammunition: Introduction of tungsten-core, armor-piercing, fin-stabilized, discarding-sabot (APFSDS) projectiles, capable of penetrating the flanks of modern main battle tanks (MBTs).

                 Thermal Management and (Sustained Fire)

• Active Liquid Cooling System (2A72 Cannons): 
• To withstand an extreme rate of fire—250 rounds per second—and a continuous throughput of 850 rounds without barrel deformation, both main cannons are equipped with closed-loop,
• forced-liquid cooling jackets, a technology derived from naval artillery systems. This drastically reduces heat buildup and prevents jams caused by overheating during prolonged engagements.

• Active Defense and Anti-Drone Electronic Warfare

• Hard-Kill Active Protection System (APS)
• Millimeter-wave flat-panel radars embedded in the sides of the turret detect incoming anti-tank missiles and loitering munitions (kamikaze drones),
• automatically firing directed fragmentation charges that intercept and destroy the threat in mid-air before impact.

                Electronic Anti-Drone Jammer (EW Jammer)
 
• A compact electronic warfare antenna installed at the rear of the turret emits targeted radio interference across multiple frequencies,
• creating a 300-meter protective bubble that severs the control and video signals of FPV drones, neutralizing vertical attacks before they reach their terminal dive angle.

                       Mobility: (enhanced)
                       
• Engine and mobility: Replace the old 780 hp V-46 engine with the 1,130 hp V-92S2F engine, providing the vehicle with the agility needed to flank enemy tanks.

              High-Speed Electric Actuators (Traverse and Elevation) 
              
• Fully Electric Turret Drive: The old, slow, and flammable hydraulic motors have been replaced by high-torque digital electric servomotors.
• This increases the turret’s rotation speed to 60° per second, allowing it to turn 180 degrees in just 3 seconds—a critical capability for responding to ambushes. 
• Two-Axis Gyroscopic Stabilization: Both the 2A72 main cannons and the two M2 Browning remote weapon stations feature independent, high-precision stabilizers, enabling accurate fire while on the move over uneven terrain. 
• Independent Servomotors for Remote Weapon Stations: The two remote turrets on the roof are equipped with dedicated, ultra-fast electric motors (capable of 90° per second traverse and up to +85° elevation).
• This ensures instantaneoustracking of FPV drones and diving aircraft without the need to rotate the entire main turret. Auxiliary Power Unit (APU): A compact, armored generator installed at the rear of the hull powers all electric motors,
• thermal imaging systems, and anti-drone jammers—even if the tank’s main engine is shut down or damaged.

                      (Rocket Saturation) 
                      
• Expanded S-8 Launchers: The side-mounted pods for 80mm unguided rockets have been resized to accommodate 15 rockets on each side (totaling 30 S-8 rockets ready for a salvo), increasing area-destruction capability by 50% compared to the original prototype.

                 (Sensors and Survivability)
                 
        Digital Driving and Navigation System (Driver's Suite) 
• Digital Periscope with Integrated Thermal Vision: Replacing the traditional optical vision block, a system of low-profile, armored cameras is installed on the glacis and the rear of the hull, featuring thermal and infrared sensors embedded directly into the armor. 
• Multifunction Display (MFD) and Augmented Reality: The driver operates the vehicle by viewing a panoramic digital screen inside the hull, which overlays navigation lines and obstacle alerts onto the thermal image of the terrain. 
• Electronic Positioning and Inertial Navigation System: Integrates a military-grade GPS/GLONASS module with an Inertial Navigation System (INS). Even if satellite signals are jammed by enemy electronic warfare, the onboard computer calculates the vehicle's precise position and terrain inclination, displaying a real-time tactical minimap on the dashboard. 
• Thermal Reversing Camera: Since T-72-based tanks have a very slow and hazardous reverse gear—compounded by a lack of rear visibility—a protected thermal camera is mounted between the rear stowage bins, allowing the driver to reverse at high speed, even through smoke screens, without needing to open the hatch. 
• PKP-M Explosive Reactive Armor (ERA): The original Kontakt-1 blocks have been entirely replaced by advanced PKP-M modules. These cover the frontal arc, hull sides, and turret, providing maximum protection against tandem-warhead threats and direct kinetic-energy impacts.
